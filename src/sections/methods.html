Section 1

Instructional Methodologies

Six complementary methods constitute the framework. Each is designed to address a distinct cognitive or motivational dimension of learning, and they are intended to be deployed in combination rather than in isolation.

M1

Multi-Sensory Conceptualisation Method

MSCM

Learning through touch, sound, and language to construct foundational concepts

MSCM replaces visual input entirely by engaging tactile, auditory, and verbal channels simultaneously. Physical objects — folded paper, segmented bars, real-world manipulables — become the primary medium through which abstract numerical relationships are made concrete.

Illustrative Example

An apple is divided into four equal portions; the student takes three. Through touch and narration the student arrives at the fraction ¾ — numerator arising from selection, denominator from the total partition.

Pedagogical Value

Conceptual understanding over rote recall Dual coding: haptic + verbal Schema formation
M2

Structured Worked Example Method

SWEM

Explicit, step-by-step modelling with embedded reasoning prompts

The instructor presents a fully worked solution accompanied by explicit narration of each procedural step, its underlying rationale, and its transferability to analogous problems. Metacognitive "why" questions are embedded throughout to foster analytical engagement.

Instructional Sequence

  • Present the problem (e.g., ½ + ⅓) and identify its category
  • Narrate each step with explicit logical justification
  • Elicit student response after each worked example
  • Generalise to problem-type pattern recognition

Pedagogical Value

Reduced extraneous cognitive load Logical reasoning scaffold Critical thinking development
M3

Guided-to-Independent Transition

GIT

Gradual release of responsibility from instructor to learner

Structured as a three-phase continuum, GIT operationalises Vygotsky's zone of proximal development by progressively withdrawing instructional scaffolding as student competence consolidates.

Three Phases

  • Phase I — Modelling: Instructor demonstrates with full narration
  • Phase II — Guided Practice: Joint problem-solving with corrective feedback
  • Phase III — Independent Practice: Student works autonomously; instructor observes

Pedagogical Value

Self-regulatory learning capacity Prevention of learned helplessness
M4

Verbalisation and Cognitive Encoding

VCE

Articulating reasoning aloud to consolidate and deepen understanding

Students are required to reconstruct and verbalise problem solutions in their own language. This transforms passive reception into active elaboration, strengthening long-term encoding and revealing gaps in comprehension that written responses would conceal.

Prompts Used in Practice

  • "Why must we find a common denominator before adding fractions?"
  • "What does the numerator represent in this context?"
  • "Can you restate the solution in your own words?"

Pedagogical Value

Procedural → conceptual transition Enhanced long-term retention
M5

Error-Based Learning Method

EBLM

Deliberate analysis of incorrect solutions as a vehicle for critical reasoning

Students are presented with worked solutions containing intentional conceptual or procedural errors and are asked to identify, diagnose, and correct them. This method activates analytical reasoning and cultivates a disposition toward mathematical scepticism.

Illustrative Error Prompt

Presented:   ½ + ⅓ = 2⁄5
Task:   Identify the error and provide the correct solution.

Pedagogical Value

Critical and evaluative thinking Misconception prevention
M6

Contextualisation Method

CM

Embedding mathematical content within meaningful real-world scenarios

Abstract operations are situated within culturally familiar and practically significant contexts, making mathematical reasoning feel purposeful and intrinsically motivated rather than arbitrary.

Illustrative Example

Financial transactions serve as the context for signed numbers: spending money corresponds to negative quantities (−1,000 VND); receiving money corresponds to positive quantities (+1,000 VND). The arithmetic emerges naturally from the lived situation.

Pedagogical Value

Intrinsic motivation Mathematical sense-making Transfer to real-world problems
