# Methodology Overview

Quick-reference summary of all six instructional methods and the Core Instructional Cycle.

---

## The Six Methods

### M1 — Multi-Sensory Conceptualisation Method (MSCM)
> *Learning through touch, sound, and language to construct foundational concepts*

Replaces visual input with tactile, auditory, and verbal channels simultaneously. Physical objects (folded paper, segmented bars) become the medium for abstract numerical relationships.

**Value:** Conceptual understanding · Dual coding · Schema formation

---

### M2 — Structured Worked Example Method (SWEM)
> *Explicit, step-by-step modelling with embedded reasoning prompts*

Instructor presents fully worked solutions with explicit narration of each step's rationale. Metacognitive "why" questions are embedded throughout.

**Value:** Reduced cognitive load · Logical reasoning scaffold · Critical thinking

---

### M3 — Guided-to-Independent Transition (GIT)
> *Gradual release of responsibility from instructor to learner*

Three phases: Modelling → Guided Practice → Independent Practice. Operationalises Vygotsky's zone of proximal development.

**Value:** Self-regulatory capacity · Prevention of learned helplessness

---

### M4 — Verbalisation and Cognitive Encoding (VCE)
> *Articulating reasoning aloud to consolidate and deepen understanding*

Students reconstruct and verbalise solutions in their own words, transforming passive reception into active elaboration.

**Value:** Procedural → conceptual transition · Enhanced long-term retention

---

### M5 — Error-Based Learning Method (EBLM)
> *Deliberate analysis of incorrect solutions as a vehicle for critical reasoning*

Students identify, diagnose, and correct intentional errors in presented solutions.

**Value:** Critical and evaluative thinking · Misconception prevention

---

### M6 — Contextualisation Method (CM)
> *Embedding mathematical content within meaningful real-world scenarios*

Abstract operations are situated in culturally familiar contexts (e.g., VND financial transactions for signed numbers).

**Value:** Intrinsic motivation · Mathematical sense-making · Transfer to real-world problems

---

## Core Instructional Cycle

A seven-phase replicable lesson structure. Phases 4–6 form an **inner loop** that repeats for each new worked example.

| Phase | Name                    | Note             |
|-------|-------------------------|------------------|
| 01    | Pre-Experience (Tactile)| —                |
| 02    | Verbal Description      | —                |
| 03    | Generalisation          | —                |
| 04 ↻  | Worked Example          | Inner loop start |
| 05 ↻  | Guided Practice         | Inner loop       |
| 06 ↻ ★| Student Explanation    | **Non-negotiable** |
| 07    | Multimedia Reinforcement| Optional         |

> Phase 6 (Student Explanation) must never be abbreviated or skipped.
